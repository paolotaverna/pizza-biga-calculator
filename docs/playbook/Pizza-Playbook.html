Paolo's direct default

9 × 270 g · 65 % · salt 2.5–3 % · fresh yeast 0.2 %
Caputo Pizzeria (W 260–290), fridge route, 24 h total, room 21 °C, fridge 4 °C. Flour 1466 g · water 924 g · salt 37 g · yeast 3.4 g. Weigh the yeast on a 0.01 g scale: a 1 g error on 3.4 g is 30 %.

Mixing on the Sunmix 6

  • Water first, at the temperature the calculator gives (heat balance: flour + water + ~6 °C of friction in 13 min). Crumble the fresh yeast into part of it until it disappears.
  • Rain the flour in over 1.5–2 min at 70–90 rpm. Then a 10–15 min covered rest (autolyse) before kneading.
  • 1st speed ~100–120 rpm, 8–10 min. Salt halfway through, never in direct contact with the yeast.
  • 2nd speed 175–200 rpm, max 2–3 min, last cold water in a stream. Stop when smooth, pulling off the bowl, "crackling".
  • Probe it: 24–25 °C off the mixer. Below 23, next time use water 3–4 °C warmer; above 25, colder. Calibrate the friction in the app from the measured value.
  • Folds: one gentle set 20 min after the mix, optional at 63–65 %, recommended above 67 % or by hand.

Why 6½ h. Balls start at ~10 °C core (the mass is ~4.5 °C after an hour out; dividing adds ~5 °C) and in a lidded box need ~3 h to get within 2 °C of a 21 °C room; the yeast works at half speed until then. Cold weighs a lot: the proof roughly doubles every 4.5 °C of colder room (~9 h at 19 °C). The mass does not cool at once either: a 2.7 kg block takes ~8 h to drop below 10 °C in the fridge, about 3 h per kg, and the real fridge is 1–2 °C below its display.

Ball weight changes only the warm-up: 350 g ≈ +½ h, 200 g ≈ −½ h. Fridge at 1 °C adds ½ h. A 24–26 °C spot (oven off with a jug of boiling water) saves ~3 h.

The probe rule

The core temperature only tells you when the proof has started, never when it is done. Once the ball is within 1 °C of the room it stops moving and the probe says nothing more.

  • Probe one ball right after balling: expect ~10 °C.
  • Note the time the core gets within 2 °C of the room (about 3 h in from a lidded box; in a 19 °C kitchen it never reaches 20 °C). Lid off with a damp towel halves that.
  • From that moment count: ~4½ h at 21 °C · ~3 h at 23 °C · ~2 h at 25 °C.
  • Then judge by volume (balls nearly doubled, low wide domes, edges touching) and the poke test: the dimple springs back slowly.
  • Put the probe on the table where the balls sit, not on the thermostat.

Dough-ball secrets

  • Tight staglio: 3–4 movements, skin under tension, seam pinched shut. The gas stays in and builds the dome.
  • Never let a skin form: containers closed for the whole proof.
  • 20/80: short bulk, long proof. The gas that puffs the rim is born inside the ball.
  • Rigenero: flat or overproofed ball? Re-ball gently, wait 45–60 min.
  • Warm balls into the oven: at 22–24 °C they explode; a cold ball stretches easily but pushes weakly.
  • Salt fully dissolved before the dough tightens.

The floor: two gradients, one landing zone

The flame climbs the left wall, rolls over the dome and the hot gases pile up at the back; cold air enters at the mouth. Hottest = back-left, coolest = front-right. Easily 80–100 °C apart.

ZoneReadingRole
Back-left (flame)480–520 °C+Finish the rim only, 5–10 s. Never launch here
Centre, slightly right, toward the mouth430–450 °CThe landing zone, middle third of the depth
Front-right (mouth)360–400 °CParking and recovery
  • Preheat at max 35–40 min before pizza 1, whatever the gun says: the 3 cm slab must charge through. A 20 min preheat gives a pale first pizza and a pale white pizza.
  • Read the exact spot 10 s before launch. Never launch below 400 °C.
  • First quarter turn at ~20 s, then two more. If the rim facing the back races, pull the pizza 5 cm toward the mouth.
  • Next pizza on fresh floor, 15–20 cm further right or forward; back to the first patch when the gun reads 430+.
  • White pizzas mid-run on a charged oven, full 80–90 s, dome the pale rims at back-left.

Longer and cooler drives off more water. Thinner centre, drained toppings, serve at once.

Dry toppings

  • Fior di latte: cut in the morning, rack or colander in the fridge uncovered (paper towel at most, never cling film). Pat dry, use cold, 70–90 g, spaced pieces.
  • Tomato: hand-crushed, 20–30 min in a sieve, salt after. 60–70 g, thin spiral, 2 cm free rim.
  • Basil and oil at the end; oil after the bake for a drier pizza.

Peel and launch

  • Dust with 50/50 semolina + rice flour, a thin veil; slap the excess off the disc. Pure rice flour carbonizes on a 430 °C biscotto and burns the base.
  • Brush the floor between pizzas: swept char is what burns the next base.
  • Even 1.5–2 cm rim, air pushed outward, thin centre. Disc to oven in under 90 s. Shake the peel; launch with a confident pull-back.
  • Balls from the fridge: 30–40 min out before stretching. Balls from a long proof at room temp: open them as they are.

Two numbers rule the night: dough at 24 °C off the mixer, floor at 430 °C on the launch spot. Measure both, every time.
